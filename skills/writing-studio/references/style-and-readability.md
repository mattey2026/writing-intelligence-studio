# Style and readability rules

## Sentences and paragraphs
- One idea per sentence. Average 14 to 20 words; no sentence over 30 words unless unavoidable.
- Mix short and medium sentences. Start sentences in varied ways.
- Active voice by default; passive under 15% of sentences.
- Plain words over jargon. Use a technical term only when the audience needs it, and explain it once.
- Paragraphs of 2 to 4 sentences. Headings plain and descriptive.

## Reading-level targets (Flesch reading ease)
| Level | Grade | Reading ease target |
|---|---|---|
| Plain | 7 to 8 | 65 or higher |
| General | 9 to 10 | 55 or higher |
| Professional | 11 to 12 | 45 or higher |
| Specialist | 13+ | 30 or higher |

## Patterns to avoid (they read as formulaic)
- Openings such as "In today's fast-paced world" or "In an era of".
- Words: delve, tapestry, testament, realm, myriad, embark, elevate, foster, unlock, harness, leverage (as a verb), seamless, robust, cutting-edge, game-changer, transformative, paradigm shift, ever-evolving, landscape (used loosely).
- Phrases: "it's important to note", "it's worth noting", "plays a crucial/pivotal role", "navigate the complexities", "at the end of the day", "in essence", "let's dive in", "here's the thing".
- Reflexive "It's not X, it's Y" and "not just X, but Y" constructions.
- Stacked rhetorical questions; reflexive lists of three.
- Furthermore, Moreover or Additionally as sentence openers.
- More than one em dash per 300 words; bold phrases inside paragraphs.
- "In conclusion" summaries and moralizing final lines.

Removing a pattern means rewriting the sentence naturally, not swapping in a synonym.

## Specificity
Replace generalities with specific claims, examples and reasoning. A sentence that could appear in any article on the topic should be cut or made specific to this one.
